Guide · Private fitness apps

Fitness apps with no social features.

There's a real, underserved audience here: people who want to train without an audience. No leaderboards, no feeds, no followers, no "kudos." This guide reviews the landscape honestly, distinguishes private by setting from private by design, and names the best app for each kind of need.

Quick answer

  • Most popular fitness apps have some social features. Strava, Nike Run Club, Peloton, MyFitnessPal: even when the social layer is optional, it's central to the product.
  • "Private by setting" is real but limited. Defaults shape behavior, and the gravity of a social product keeps pulling. The cleanest privacy is the one you don't have to configure.
  • "Private by design" apps have no social layer to opt out of. The category is small but growing.
  • For a private AI coach: Justus. Zero social features, by construction.
  • For workout videos, privately: Apple Fitness+.
  • For private strength logging: Fitbod, Strong, or Hevy. Light or no social layer.
  • For activity tracking with social turned off: Garmin Connect on a Garmin device.

The honest list, ranked by how private.

Sorted from "private by design" (no social layer at all) to "private by setting" (social exists; you can opt out). The framing matters more than the ranking — your right pick depends on what you want the app to do.

Justus

Private by design · AI coach

What it is: A private AI fitness coach. Conversational planning across all modalities (run, lift, swim, mobility), persistent memory across years, adapts to real life. Built on the principle that fitness shouldn't come with an audience.

Social layer: None. No feed, no followers, no leaderboards, no kudos, no "friends." That isn't a setting; it's the architecture.

Best for: people who want a coach instead of a feed; anyone who's been burned by social comparison.

Apple Fitness+

Private by default · Workout videos

What it is: Apple's subscription library of workout video content. You pick a class, the trainer leads you through, your ring data updates on your watch.

Social layer: Minimal. You can optionally share your activity rings with people you choose. There's no feed, no leaderboard, no followers.

Best for: people who want guided workout videos and already live in the Apple ecosystem.

Fitbod

Mostly private · Strength logger

What it is: An algorithmic strength training app that builds your next workout based on what you've done. Strong progression logic for lifters.

Social layer: Light. No feed or followers. You log your own training and the algorithm plans the next session.

Best for: lifters who want a smart progression engine without a community attached.

Strong / Hevy

Mostly private · Strength loggers

What they are: Clean strength-training loggers. Track sets, reps, and weight; chart progress over time. Hevy has an optional social feed; Strong is even more utilitarian.

Social layer: Optional in Hevy (you can ignore it). Effectively absent in Strong.

Best for: lifters who want a manual logger without algorithms or community.

Garmin Connect

Private by setting · Activity tracker

What it is: The companion app for Garmin watches. Strong tracking, detailed metrics, and the long-standing Garmin Coach feature for running.

Social layer: Real but optional. Challenges, groups, and a feed exist; you can choose not to add connections and keep everything private.

Best for: people on a Garmin who want metrics without joining the community side of the platform.

Private by design vs private by setting

The difference matters. Private by setting means social features exist; you've just turned them off. The platform is still optimized for engagement, the defaults still pull toward sharing, and the gravity of the product works against you staying private.

Private by design means there's nothing to opt out of. No feed exists to mute. No followers to remove. No leaderboards to ignore. The product simply doesn't have a social layer. That's a different kind of quiet.

What you give up by going private

Honestly? Some real things. Social fitness apps work for a lot of people, and there's evidence that public accountability and group encouragement help with adherence. If a Strava ride with your friends is the reason you actually get on the bike, that's a feature, not a flaw.

The case for private apps isn't that social is bad. It's that social isn't for everyone, and for the people it doesn't serve, there should be apps that take their preference seriously rather than treating "private" as a checkbox.

How to pick, in three questions

1. Do you want a coach or a recorder? If coaching is the job, Justus is the cleanest answer in the private category. If you just want to record, Apple Fitness+, Garmin Connect, or a strength logger will do it.

2. Do you want one app or several? Most private apps are single-purpose: strength logger, workout videos, activity tracker. Justus is the unusual one in that it covers multiple modalities in a single coordinated plan.

3. How much do you want to think about privacy settings? If the answer is "never again," a privacy-by-design product is worth more than a privacy-by-setting one, even when they're functionally similar today.

Common questions about private fitness apps.

What's the most private fitness app?

Apps that are private by design (with no social features at all) are the cleanest answer. Justus is built that way: zero social features, by construction. Apple Fitness+ is private by default for workout content. Garmin Connect, Fitbod, Strong, and Hevy can be used privately if you don't add friends.

The cleanest privacy is the one you don't have to configure.

Is there a fitness app with no leaderboards?

Yes. Justus has no leaderboards as a design principle. Apple Fitness+ shows you only your own ring data. Strength loggers like Fitbod, Strong, and Hevy don't include leaderboards as a core feature.

Strava and most running apps build their product around leaderboards and segments, so they're not the right pick if you specifically want to avoid them.

Can I use Strava without anyone seeing my activity?

You can set your default activity privacy to followers-only or private. The platform supports it, but the product is designed around social activity, so the defaults and the friction work against staying private.

If your goal is to never have to think about who's watching, a privacy-by-design app is a cleaner fit.

Is Apple Fitness+ private?

Yes, by default. Apple Fitness+ is workout video content; you watch sessions and your ring data updates. There's no feed, no followers, and no leaderboards.

The only optional social layer is sharing your activity rings with specific people you choose, which is off by default.

What's the best fitness app without social features?

It depends on what you want. For a private AI coach that plans across multiple sports, Justus. For workout videos, Apple Fitness+. For private strength logging, Fitbod, Strong, or Hevy. For private activity tracking on a Garmin device, Garmin Connect with social features off.

The framing matters: most apps offer "private mode" as a setting, but a few are built private from the start.
