The best fitness app for multi-sport training.

If you run and lift and swim, you've probably been juggling three apps that don't know about each other. That's not a personal failure; it's the state of the market. This guide reviews the small set of apps that actually coordinate across modalities, and how to think about picking one.

The single-sport app problem

Fitness apps grew up around individual sports. Strava and Runna for running. Strong, Hevy, and Fitbod for lifting. TrainerRoad and Zwift for cycling. Each is excellent at its thing. None of them really reasons about the others.

For someone who only runs, this is fine. For everyone else, the coordination problem becomes a chore: you're the integrator. You're holding the picture of how Tuesday's lift, Wednesday's swim, and Saturday's long run fit together. The apps aren't.

Why interference matters

Training stress isn't compartmentalized. A heavy back-squat session leaves your legs trashed for 24–48 hours, which makes a long run the next morning a bad idea. A long swim can fatigue your aerobic system enough to mess with your tempo run two days later. These interactions are real, and they're invisible to any app that only sees one sport.

A multimodal coach knows about both sides and plans accordingly. The lift you did yesterday is part of the input to tomorrow's run plan.

What multimodal periodization actually means

Periodization — the long arc of base, build, peak, taper — works for one sport pretty cleanly. For two or three sports trained together, the arcs have to be reconciled. Most apps avoid this by making one sport "primary" and the rest "supplementary."

A real multimodal coach reconciles the arcs. The strength plan deloads when the run plan is peaking. The mobility work moves to match. The whole calendar is one document, not three.

Honest tradeoffs of multimodal apps

You give up depth for breadth. A specialist app will go deeper on its sport than a multimodal app can. If you're a serious triathlete chasing a specific Ironman time, TriDot will outperform Justus on the tri-specific structures. If you're a competitive lifter targeting a meet, a specialist coach will outperform any general app on programming.

The tradeoff: most people aren't on those edges. Most people are juggling 2–4 modalities and just want them to coordinate. Breadth is what they need.

Common questions about multi-sport training apps.

What's the best fitness app for triathletes?

For serious triathletes targeting specific races: TriDot and TrainingPeaks are the category standards. Both are built around structured tri training.

For triathletes who also want their training to coordinate with strength work, mobility, sleep, and life, Justus is a broader fit. The right answer depends on whether you're race-focused or training year-round across modalities.

What app plans both running and strength?

Most apps treat one as primary and the other as supplementary. Runna and Nike Run Club include strength sessions but design around the running plan. Strength loggers like Fitbod and Strong include cardio but design around lifting.

Justus is one of the few apps built to program running and strength as peers — neither is the "extra" sport.
