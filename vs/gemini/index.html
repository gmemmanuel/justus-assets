Compare · Justus vs Gemini

Gemini can write you a plan. It can't really coach you.

Gemini is one of the most capable general-purpose AI assistants available, with a long context window and strong multimodal reasoning. For drafting a workout plan or thinking through a training question, it's excellent. There's still a real gap between drafting a plan and coaching someone through one. This page is an honest look at where Gemini falls short for fitness, and what a dedicated AI coach does differently.

Quick answer

  • Gemini can draft a workout plan. Tell it your goal, your schedule, your constraints, and it'll write something thoughtful. The long context window lets you paste a lot of data for a more grounded first draft.
  • It has no persistent memory of your training. Tomorrow it's a stranger again. The memory feature holds a handful of facts; it doesn't hold your plan.
  • It can't see your data. No watch sync, no plan you log against, no awareness that you did intervals on Tuesday unless you remember to mention it.
  • It can't follow up. Coaching is a conversation that continues. Gemini only responds when you open it.
  • Best fit for Gemini: one-off questions, drafting starting plans, working through training concepts, leveraging the long context window to paste large amounts of data.
  • Best fit for Justus: a coach that holds the plan, sees your data, remembers you, and adapts in real time.
  • The honest take: Gemini is brilliant. It's just not designed to be a coach. Different job.

Who each one is actually for.

Gemini and a dedicated AI fitness coach aren't the same product in different packaging; they're different products entirely. Here's the most honest version of who picks which.

Pick Gemini if

You want a smart general-purpose helper

  • You're happy managing your own plan and just want a starting point
  • You have one specific question (form, exercise swap, sets/reps math)
  • You don't mind re-explaining yourself each session
  • You already live in Google's ecosystem and like having one tool
  • You don't want or need a relationship with a coach

Pick Justus if

You want an actual coach

  • You want a plan that exists between sessions, not one you redraft each time
  • You want your watch data to actually shape the plan
  • You want to be checked on, not just respond when you remember to ask
  • You train across modalities (run, lift, swim, mobility) and want them coordinated
  • You want a coach that knows you across years, not the next 20 minutes

What Gemini is genuinely great at

Gemini is one of the most capable models available. For fitness, it's excellent at: handling large amounts of context (paste a year of workout logs and ask for trends), multimodal questions (a screenshot of your watch metrics, a photo of an exercise machine), drafting starting plans (give me a 4-day upper/lower split for a 35-year-old beginner), and quick math (estimate my 1RM from this set).

If you're a self-directed athlete who likes managing your own training and wants a smart sparring partner, Gemini is more than capable.

Where it falls short: no memory of your training

Gemini's memory feature can hold a handful of facts ("user is a 35-year-old runner, training for a half marathon, has a sore left knee"). What it can't hold is the actual plan: the sessions you've done, the ones coming up, your trend lines, your last conversation about how your sleep has been.

Every session is mostly a cold start. Even with Gems and the memory feature, you're maintaining context yourself. A coach holds the plan so you don't have to.

The stranger every time problem

A real coach builds a model of you over months and years. They know that your knee acts up after high-mileage weeks, that you get nervous before race blocks, that you're more honest about recovery on Sunday nights than Monday mornings. That kind of knowledge accumulates.

With Gemini, every conversation starts close to zero. You can paste a summary, but it's a summary, not a relationship. Justus is designed to build the relationship.

Coaching is more than the plan

People think they want a plan. Mostly what they want is a coach. The plan is the artifact. The coach is the thing that changes the plan when life happens. The plan without the adaptation is just a worksheet.

Gemini will draft you a great worksheet. The first time you miss a Tuesday and skip a Saturday, the worksheet is wrong, and there's no one to fix it. Justus reshapes the plan when you tell it what happened — without you having to redraft anything.

Can you make Gemini your coach with workarounds?

Sort of. You can build a Gem with custom instructions, paste your data each week, keep the plan in a Google Doc, and run a weekly check-in conversation. Some people do this and get value from it.

What breaks: data ingestion is manual, the plan is a document you have to read and remember, there's no follow-up unless you initiate it, and the model still re-anchors on whatever's in the prompt rather than building on your real history. You become the plumbing. An app like Justus exists so you don't have to be.

When to use which, honestly

Use Gemini for the one-off stuff: a starting plan, an exercise you've never tried, parsing a complicated screenshot of your watch data. It's a great sparring partner.

Use Justus (or any purpose-built AI coach) when you want the plan, the memory, the adaptation, and the follow-up bundled into a single product designed for the job. Different tools for different jobs.

Common questions about this comparison.

Can Gemini actually build me a workout plan?

Yes, it can draft one. Tell Gemini your goal, your schedule, and your constraints, and it'll produce a structured plan that's roughly in line with the evidence. Gemini's long context window means you can paste a lot of data — past workouts, a screenshot of your watch — and get a more grounded first draft.

The catch is that the next time you open Gemini, the conversation is gone. The plan only updates if you remember to re-explain everything.

Can Gemini be my personal trainer?

For one-off questions, yes. For ongoing coaching, no. A trainer holds your plan, sees your data, remembers your history, and follows up. Gemini does none of those by default.

Gems (custom personas) and the memory feature in Google's ecosystem let you fake parts of it, but the system isn't designed to be a coach with a structured plan, wearable sync, and proactive follow-up.

Is there an AI fitness coach that's better than Gemini for fitness?

Yes. Apps built specifically as AI coaches hold a persistent profile, plug into your wearable data, maintain an actual plan, and follow up over time. Justus is built that way.

Gemini is a general-purpose assistant; coaching is something it can roleplay but not actually do.

Is Justus built on Gemini?

Justus is a multi-agent system designed specifically for fitness coaching: persistent profile, plan maintenance, multimodal training orchestration, and safety guardrails.

The underlying language models can evolve as the technology evolves — what makes Justus a coach is the architecture around the model, not which model is in the box.

Can I make Gemini remember my workouts?

Partly. Gemini has a memory feature that holds a few facts about you, and Gems (custom personas) let you keep a workspace with persistent instructions.

Neither is a substitute for a coach that holds your actual plan, sees your watch data, and writes to you when something needs attention. The architecture is built for general assistance, not longitudinal coaching.
