Compare · Justus vs Strava

Strava records what you did. Justus plans what's next.

Strava is the dominant fitness social network and one of the best activity recorders ever built. Justus is something different: a private AI coach that holds your plan, learns your history, and adapts across years. Here's an honest comparison.

Quick answer

  • Strava is a fitness social network. Its core job is recording GPS-tracked workouts, sharing them with followers, and competing on segments. It's industry-standard for runners and cyclists.
  • Justus is a private AI fitness coach. Its core job is planning your training, adapting around your life, and remembering you over years. Zero social features.
  • Strava doesn't really coach. Premium adds training plans and a Fitness & Freshness score, but there's no conversation, no memory across years, and no multi-sport orchestration.
  • Justus doesn't really socialize. No feeds, no followers, no leaderboards. By design.
  • Best fit for Strava: cyclists, runners, and anyone who trains better with public accountability.
  • Best fit for Justus: people who want a coach instead of a feed; multi-sport athletes; anyone who's been burned by rigid plans or social comparison.
  • The honest take: they aren't actually competitors. Many people will pair them — Strava for recording and community, Justus for planning and memory.

Who each one is actually for.

The framing "Strava vs Justus" suggests a head-to-head. In practice, they answer different questions. Here's the most honest version of who picks which.

Pick Strava if

The community is the product

  • You're a road or gravel cyclist; the segment culture is the sport for you
  • You train better when your runs are public
  • You already have a riding or running group on Strava
  • You want the best activity recording in the category
  • You care more about logging what happened than planning what's next

Pick Justus if

The coach is the product

  • You want guidance, not a feed
  • You train across multiple sports (run, lift, swim, mobility)
  • You've quietly bounced off Strava because the social side felt like pressure
  • Your training has to bend around real life: travel, sleep, injury, family
  • You want a coach who remembers you across years, not sessions

Recording vs planning

Strava is the recorder. Press start on your watch, finish the run, and Strava lays it out beautifully: pace splits, elevation, heart-rate zones, segment efforts, kudos from your friends. It's one of the cleanest activity views ever built.

What Strava doesn't really do is tell you what to do tomorrow. Premium offers structured training plans, but they're templates, not a coach. They don't know you had a bad night of sleep, that your knee was sore on Tuesday, or that you have a wedding on Saturday. Justus does the opposite: it builds and maintains your plan, and the recording lives on your watch.

Public by default vs private by design

Strava's central pitch is the social network. Activities are shareable to followers, segment leaderboards are global, and the product is optimized for engagement with other athletes. You can adjust privacy settings, but the gravity of the platform pulls toward public.

Justus is the opposite. No leaderboards, no feed, no followers, no kudos. Your training is a conversation between you and a coach. That isn't a setting; it's the entire design of the product. Just us.

One activity at a time vs multimodal

Strava records activities, but each one is its own thing. Tuesday's lift sits next to Saturday's long run in your history; nothing in the product is reasoning across them. If you also swim, do mobility work, or alternate cycles between lifting and running, you're stitching the picture together yourself.

Justus programs across modalities in a single plan. Your long run won't land the day after a heavy lower-body session. Your swim sits in the right place relative to the rest of the week. That's not a "feature"; it's the only way Justus knows how to plan.

A score vs a conversation

Strava's coaching layer is a number. Fitness & Freshness gives you a trend line, and Premium plans hand you a workout each day. If you miss one, the plan doesn't reshape; you just fall behind it.

Justus's coaching layer is a conversation. Tell it your knee is sore and the plan adjusts. Tell it you had a brutal week at work and it lightens tomorrow. Tell it you want to add a half marathon in October and it re-periodizes around the rest of your training. The plan bends around your life, not the other way around.

How each one makes its money

Strava is a subscription business with a social-network engagement loop on top. Premium runs $11.99/month or $79.99/year. The free tier is intentionally pared back, and the platform's growth depends on social activity, which colors every product decision they make.

Justus is a subscription business and that's it. No ads, no social loop to optimize, no data sold to third parties. Pricing for the research preview is intentionally low while we figure out what a private AI coach should feel like. The product succeeds when you train better, not when you scroll more.

So which one should you actually use?

Honestly? For most people who care about this comparison, the answer is probably both. Record on your watch, let Strava handle the social and post-activity polish, and let Justus handle the planning, the memory, and the adaptation.

If you have to pick one and the social side has stopped serving you, choose Justus. If you don't really want a coach and the community is the whole point, stay on Strava. There's no shame in either answer; they're just different products.

Common questions about this comparison.

Is Strava better than Justus?

They're built for different jobs. Strava is the best fitness social network and one of the best activity recorders in the category. Justus is a private AI coach that plans your training. Strava is better if you want a community feed and segment leaderboards. Justus is better if you want guidance, privacy, and a plan that adapts across years.

What's a good Strava alternative if I don't want the social features?

Justus is the most direct answer: it's designed as the opposite of a fitness social network. No leaderboards, no feeds, no followers. Your training stays between you and your coach.

Other private alternatives include Apple Fitness+ and Garmin Connect, but they're recorders and players, not coaches. If you want to record privately and not be coached, those are reasonable picks too.

Does Justus have GPS tracking like Strava?

Justus reads GPS-tracked workouts through Apple Health and Health Connect, so anything recorded on a watch (Apple Watch, Garmin, Coros, Polar, Suunto) flows in automatically. The recording itself happens on your watch or phone; Justus uses it to build and adapt your plan.

Can I use both Strava and Justus?

Yes, and many people will. Record your runs and rides on your watch, post the ones you want to Strava for the community side, and let Justus plan and adapt the training. The two don't overlap much, which is the point of comparing them at all.

What does Justus cost compared to Strava?

Strava Premium is $11.99/month or $79.99/year. There's a free tier with reduced features. Justus pricing isn't final, but the research preview is intentionally low-cost for early users. Final pricing will be announced before public launch.
