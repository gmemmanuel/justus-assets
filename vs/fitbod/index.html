Fitbod is the best at picking your next lift. Justus programs the rest of your training too.

Fitbod is one of the cleanest strength apps ever built. Its algorithm picks your next workout based on what you've done, and the progression logic is sharp. The catch is that strength is one modality, and most people train across more than one. This page is an honest comparison.

Who each one is actually for.

What Fitbod is genuinely great at

Fitbod is one of the cleanest pieces of fitness software in the category. The exercise selection algorithm is sensible, the progression logic respects fatigue, and the UX is purpose-built for the gym — log a set, see the next one, move on. For someone who only lifts and wants the app to decide what they're doing today, Fitbod is hard to beat.

The strength-specific features (alternative-exercise suggestions, plate calculator, warm-up sets, fresh muscle groups) are all sharper than what a generalist coach would build. It's a specialist tool, and it does its job well.

The "strength-only" trade-off

Fitbod is strength-only by design. That's not a knock; it's the product's intentional scope. The problem only shows up when your training isn't strength-only.

If you also run, swim, cycle, or play a sport, Fitbod doesn't know about any of that. It picks your next lift assuming yesterday's heavy squats are the only thing in your legs — not the 10-miler you also did. You become the integrator, holding the picture of how all your training fits together. Justus is built so you don't have to.

Algorithm vs conversation

Fitbod's intelligence is algorithmic. You feed it data (sets, reps, weights, fatigue ratings) and it produces an output (your next workout). The conversation, such as it is, happens through buttons and toggles.

Justus's intelligence is conversational. You can say "my knee felt off on yesterday's squats, can we lighten today's session," and the plan reshapes. An algorithm can't handle the cases the designer didn't anticipate. A conversation can.

Recovery awareness

Fitbod uses your own logged fatigue and muscle-group recency to shape the next session. That's better than nothing, but it's blind to everything outside the strength session: how you slept, how recovered you are systemically, whether your other training is stressing the same systems.

Justus reads sleep, HRV, training load, and movement data from Apple Health or Health Connect, and the plan responds. The lift you did yesterday isn't the only thing in your body when today's plan is built.

The "I've outgrown Fitbod" moment

A common arc: someone uses Fitbod for 6–18 months, gets comfortable with strength training, and then starts running, or picks up a sport, or wants to add mobility work. Suddenly the strength-only app is fighting against the rest of their training rather than supporting it.

This is the moment a multimodal coach starts to outperform a strength specialist. Fitbod isn't built to know about anything else. Justus is.

So which one should you actually use?

If lifting is the whole sport for you and you don't see that changing, stay on Fitbod. Its algorithm is genuinely good and the friction of switching isn't worth it for what you'd gain.

If you're already training across modalities, or you can see yourself doing that in the next year, the multimodal coach is the better long-term home. Different products, different jobs.

The hybrid-athlete question: where does strength fit in the race block?

Fitbod is useful when the lifting session is the main event. The harder problem for a runner, cyclist, swimmer, or triathlete is deciding how much strength belongs beside intervals, long sessions, and recovery. Justus treats lifting as part of the same event plan, so a hard lower-body day does not accidentally compete with the workout that matters most that week.

Common questions about this comparison.

What's a good Fitbod alternative?

If you want another algorithmic strength logger, Caliber and Boostcamp are reasonable alternatives. If you want a manual logger with less algorithm, Strong and Hevy are the classics.

If you want a coach that programs strength alongside running, swimming, and mobility in one plan, Justus is the right fit. Fitbod's core competence is strength selection; Justus is a coach across modalities.

Is Fitbod good for hybrid athletes?

Not really. Fitbod is excellent if you only lift, but it's a strength-only app by design. It doesn't reason about your runs, your sleep, your recovery, or the rest of your training.

If you also run, swim, or do mobility work, you're running Fitbod alongside other apps and coordinating manually. Apps built for multi-sport athletes — like Justus — program strength as part of a coordinated plan.

Does Justus program strength training?

Yes. Justus programs strength with the same care as any other modality: progressive overload, sensible session structure, appropriate volume and intensity, deload weeks.

The difference from Fitbod is that the strength plan is coordinated with everything else you train — your run plan informs your lift plan and vice versa. The lift you did yesterday actually shapes today's run, and your run yesterday shapes today's lift.

How much does Fitbod cost vs Justus?

Fitbod offers paid monthly and annual plans. Justus pricing isn't final; the research preview is paid and priced below the planned standard rate.

The comparison isn't apples-to-apples — Fitbod is a strength logger, Justus is a multimodal coach — but for users training across modalities, replacing 3 single-sport subscriptions with 1 coach usually nets out lower.

Should I use Fitbod or Justus?

Use Fitbod if you only lift and you want a sharp algorithm picking your next workout. It's purpose-built for that job.

Use Justus if you train across modalities, if you want a conversation rather than an algorithm, or if you want a coach that holds your plan across years instead of session-by-session. Different products for different jobs.
