What Strong is genuinely great at

Strong is one of the cleanest pieces of fitness software ever made. The design philosophy is that the app should stay out of the way: open it, see your routine, log your sets, close it. No community feed pulling for engagement, no algorithm second-guessing your choices, no AI trying to coach you.

For experienced lifters who already know what they're doing, this is exactly the right product. Strong respects that you know what you want.

The "already knows what they want" assumption

Strong is built around the assumption that you walked into the gym with a program in mind. You picked it (or wrote it), and the app's job is to record what happened. The app doesn't try to teach you what to do.

That assumption holds for some people. For most, especially newer or generalist trainees, the question of "what should I do today" is harder than the question of "did I do my 5 sets." Justus answers the first question. Strong assumes you already answered it.

Logger vs coach

This is the whole difference. A logger captures what happened. A coach decides what should happen next. They're separate products solving separate problems.

Some people only need a logger. Some people only need a coach. Some people use both — a coach to write the plan, a logger to record what they did against it. Most people who want both end up using one app that does both, which is closer to what Justus is built to be.

What Strong deliberately doesn't do

The product's restraint is its strength. No social feed, so no comparison pressure. No algorithm, so no algorithmic surprises. No AI suggestions, so no over-the-top "the AI thinks you should..." moments.

Some people read that list and think "exactly what I want." Others read it and think "I actually do want some of those." Both are fine. Strong is honest about what it is.

How to know you've outgrown Strong

Two signals. First: you find yourself spending more time figuring out what to do than logging what you did. The product is built around the latter, not the former. Second: you start training outside of lifting — running, cycling, mobility, sport — and the logger isn't reasoning about any of it.

Those are coaching needs, and a logger isn't built for them. That's the moment Justus starts to make sense.

So which one should you actually use?

If you have a program you trust and you just want to log it, Strong is still the right answer. There's no reason to add complexity you don't need.

If you want the app to also program your training, hold the plan across years, coordinate with the rest of your life, and adapt when things change — Strong isn't trying to do any of that. Justus is.

Common questions about this comparison.

What's a good Strong app alternative?

If you want another minimalist strength logger, Hevy is the closest direct alternative. If you want algorithmic exercise selection rather than manual logging, Fitbod is the move.

If you want a coach that programs your training rather than a notepad to record it, Justus is the right fit. Strong is intentionally a logger; it doesn't try to coach.

Is Strong app still good in 2026?

Yes, for what it is. Strong is a clean, opinionated strength logger with no community features and no algorithm. If you know what you want to do and just need to log it, Strong does that job as well as anything.

It hasn't changed much, and that's intentional — the design philosophy is that the app should stay out of the way.

What does Strong cost?

Strong is free to use with a limited number of saved workouts. Strong Pro is about $4.99/month or $29.99/year and unlocks unlimited workouts and additional features.

The pricing has remained consistently low because the product is genuinely simple.

Does Justus replace Strong?

It depends on what you use Strong for. If you use Strong as a pure log because you already know what you want to do, Justus is a different product — it's a coach, not a logger.

If you find yourself wishing the logger would also tell you what to do, plan your week, or coordinate with your other training, that's where Justus comes in. Many lifters who outgrow "just log it" end up wanting a coach.

Strong vs Hevy vs Justus — which one?

Strong and Hevy are similar utilitarian loggers; the main difference is that Hevy has an optional social layer. Both are designed to record what you did, not coach what you do next.

Justus is a private AI coach that programs and adapts your plan across modalities. Pick a logger if you want a notepad; pick Justus if you want a coach.
