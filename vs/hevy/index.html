What Hevy is genuinely great at

Hevy is a polished strength logger with a real community. The interface is clean, the routine builder is good, the progression charts are useful, and the feed gives lifters a place to see what their friends are doing. For people who like having that social layer attached to their training, Hevy hits the brief.

The premium tier adds advanced analytics, rest-timer customizations, and unlimited routines. It's a reasonable upgrade for serious lifters who want more from the logging side.

The "optional social" trade-off

Hevy's social layer is technically optional — you can choose not to add friends and keep workouts off the feed. The catch is that the product is designed around the social side, so the defaults nudge toward sharing and the gravity of the platform pulls in that direction.

If you specifically want to avoid social fitness, "optional" isn't the same as "absent." Justus is privacy by design: no feed exists to mute, no followers to manage, no leaderboards to ignore. Different design philosophy.

Logger vs coach

Hevy is a logger. Its job is to record what you did and let you (or the community) see the trend. Justus is a coach. Its job is to plan what you should do next and adapt the plan when life happens.

Some people want both, separately. Most people who want both end up using one app that does both. Justus is closer to one app that does both.

Strength-only vs multimodal

Hevy is strength-only. The product makes no claim to track or coordinate other modalities, and that's a deliberate scope choice. For lifters who only lift, that's fine.

For the increasing number of trainees who lift AND run AND do mobility work AND occasionally swim, a strength-only product becomes one of several. Justus collapses all of them into one coordinated plan. The lift you did yesterday actually informs today's run.

Recovery awareness

Hevy can read some workout data through Apple Health, but it doesn't really build coaching decisions on top of recovery signals. The product structure isn't designed around adapting your next session to how you slept or how recovered you are systemically.

Justus reads sleep, HRV, and training load and the plan responds. Less load when you're under-recovered; more when you're not.

So which one should you actually use?

If you want a strength logger and you like having a community attached, Hevy is well-built for that. There's nothing wrong with the product.

If you want privacy as a default, a coach instead of a notepad, and a plan that coordinates across the rest of your training — Hevy isn't built for any of that. Justus is.

Common questions about this comparison.

What's a good Hevy alternative?

If you want another strength logger without the social layer, Strong is the closest minimalist alternative. If you want algorithmic exercise selection rather than manual logging, Fitbod is the move.

If you want a coach that programs and adapts your training across modalities, Justus is the right fit. Hevy's social layer is optional but real; Justus has no social layer at all.

Is Hevy private?

You can make Hevy mostly private by not adding friends and keeping your workouts off the feed, but the social layer is a real part of the product. The defaults pull toward sharing, and the platform's growth depends on social activity.

If your goal is to train without ever thinking about who might see your workouts, a privacy-by-design app is a cleaner fit.

Does Hevy have a coach feature?

Hevy includes routine templates and progression suggestions within the logging flow, but it isn't a coaching app. The product is built around recording what you did and sharing it.

It doesn't hold a long-term plan, doesn't coordinate with other modalities, and doesn't adapt to your sleep, recovery, or life circumstances the way a coach would.

What does Hevy cost vs Justus?

Hevy is free for the basics and around $4.99–$6.99/month or $39.99/year for Hevy Pro. Justus pricing isn't final, but the research preview is intentionally low-cost for early users.

The two products do different jobs — Hevy is a logger with community, Justus is a coach without social — so the comparison isn't apples-to-apples.
