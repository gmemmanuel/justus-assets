What TrainingPeaks is genuinely great at

TrainingPeaks is the deepest plan editor and analytics platform in endurance sports. The structured-workout builder is unmatched, the watch integrations are mature, and the training-stress modeling (TSS, CTL, ATL, the whole Coggan vocabulary) has been the language of endurance coaching for a generation. For a serious endurance athlete with a coach who writes plans in it, the combination is extremely effective.

It's also the de facto standard for endurance coaches. If you hire a triathlon coach, an endurance running coach, or a cycling coach, there's a high chance they'll deliver your plan through TrainingPeaks. That's a real network effect.

The "intelligence is in the coach" point

This is the honest framing TrainingPeaks itself wouldn't disagree with. The platform is a tool. The thinking — periodization, adaptation, watching for overreach, balancing the week — comes from whoever is authoring the plan. With a good coach, it's brilliant. Without one, you're the coach.

That isn't a knock; it's the design. TrainingPeaks's job is to be the best place for a plan to live, not to write the plan itself. Justus is built around the opposite premise: the coach is the product, and the plan emerges from the conversation.

Editor vs coach

The split is roughly: TrainingPeaks is excellent if you have a human coach or you're confident self-coaching. Justus is excellent if you want the AI to be the coach and don't want to manage a structured-workout editor by hand.

Hybrid workflows exist (Justus builds the plan, you log against TP for analytics), but in practice running two training systems creates friction. Most people end up picking the one that matches who's authoring the plan.

Endurance-deep vs multimodal

TrainingPeaks's heritage is endurance — cycling, triathlon, running. Strength work is supported, but it's a supplementary track in a product whose center of gravity is structured endurance work. The deep analytics are most useful for endurance-specific training.

Justus treats strength as a peer modality to running, swimming, cycling, and mobility. The coordination across modalities is the central design choice rather than an add-on. For a runner who also lifts seriously, that breadth matters.

The analytics trade-off

TrainingPeaks exposes more metrics than almost any other platform. TSS, IF, CTL, ATL, TSB, power curves, fatigue/freshness charts. If you love the numbers, the product respects that and shows you everything.

Justus uses similar underlying signals but doesn't expose all of them as the main interface. The bet is that most people want a coach who reads the data and makes good decisions, not a dashboard they have to interpret themselves. If you're an analytics person, TP is the deeper home.

The coach-on-the-other-end question

One thing TrainingPeaks does that no AI coach replicates: it puts a specific human on the other end of your plan. For some people, that human relationship is the entire point of coaching, and a TrainingPeaks + coach setup is what they want.

Justus is intentionally AI-only. The bet is that a well-designed AI coach with persistent memory and structured tools serves most people most of the time at software pricing — without the cost or scheduling overhead of a human in the loop. If the human relationship is what you specifically want, TP + a coach is the better fit.

So which one should you actually use?

If you have a human coach (or want one), and especially if you're an endurance-focused athlete, TrainingPeaks plus that coach is hard to beat. The platform has earned its position in the endurance world.

If you want the coach to be AI — with conversational adaptation, multimodal planning, and persistent memory — Justus is the cleaner answer. You skip the editor entirely; the coach holds the plan. Different shapes for different needs.

Common questions about this comparison.

What's the difference between TrainingPeaks and Justus?

TrainingPeaks is a plan editor and analytics platform — by default, the intelligence comes from a human coach who writes the plan inside it, or from you self-coaching.

Justus is the coach: the AI builds and maintains the plan, adapts it conversationally, and reads your wearable data. TrainingPeaks is the canvas; Justus is the coach holding the brush.

Is TrainingPeaks worth it without a coach?

It can be, if you self-coach. TrainingPeaks gives you the structured-workout builder, analytics, calendar, and watch sync that serious endurance athletes use.

But you're authoring the plan yourself, which is real work — that's where most users end up either hiring a coach or moving to a product where the coaching is built in. Justus is one of the products where the coaching is built in.

How much does TrainingPeaks cost?

TrainingPeaks has a free tier and a Premium tier around $19.99/month (or $119.99/year). A human coach using TrainingPeaks typically costs separately, usually $100–$300+ per month depending on the coach.

Justus pricing isn't final, but the research preview is intentionally low-cost for early users.

Can I use TrainingPeaks and Justus together?

In theory, yes — Justus can build the plan and you could log against TrainingPeaks for the analytics. In practice, running two training systems usually causes more friction than it's worth.

If you want a human coach in the loop, TrainingPeaks plus a coach is the established path. If you want an AI coach end-to-end, Justus is built for that and doesn't require a second tool.

Should I use TrainingPeaks or Justus?

Use TrainingPeaks if you have (or want) a human coach who'll write your plan inside it. The combination is hard to beat for serious endurance athletes.

Use Justus if you want an AI coach that handles the planning and adaptation itself, especially if you train across modalities and don't want to manage a structured-workout editor by hand. Different shapes for different needs.
