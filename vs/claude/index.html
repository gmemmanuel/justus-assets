Compare · Justus vs Claude

Claude can write you a plan. It can't really coach you.

Claude is one of the most capable general-purpose AI assistants out there. For drafting a workout plan, explaining training concepts, or thinking through a nuanced question, it's excellent. There's still a real gap between drafting a plan and coaching someone through one. This page is an honest look at where Claude falls short for fitness, and what a dedicated AI coach does differently.

Quick answer

  • Claude can draft a workout plan. Tell it your goal, your schedule, your constraints, and it'll write something thoughtful and well-reasoned. Claude is particularly strong on nuance.
  • It has no persistent memory of your training. Tomorrow it's a stranger again. Even with Projects, you're maintaining the context yourself.
  • It can't see your data. No watch sync, no plan you log against, no awareness that you did intervals on Tuesday unless you remember to mention it.
  • It can't follow up. Coaching is a conversation that continues. Claude only responds when you open it.
  • Best fit for Claude: nuanced one-off questions, deep reasoning about your training, drafting a starting plan you'll manage yourself, working through an injury question with care.
  • Best fit for Justus: a coach that holds the plan, sees your data, remembers you, and adapts in real time.
  • The honest take: Claude is brilliant. It's just not designed to be a coach. Different job.

Who each one is actually for.

Claude and a dedicated AI fitness coach aren't the same product in different packaging; they're different products entirely. Here's the most honest version of who picks which.

Pick Claude if

You want a thoughtful sparring partner

  • You're happy managing your own plan and just want careful reasoning when you ask for it
  • You have specific questions where nuance matters (a calf strain, a deload week, a periodization question)
  • You don't mind re-explaining yourself each session (or keeping a Projects workspace)
  • You already use Claude for other work and like having one tool
  • You don't want or need a relationship with a coach

Pick Justus if

You want an actual coach

  • You want a plan that exists between sessions, not one you redraft each time
  • You want your watch data to actually shape the plan
  • You want to be checked on, not just respond when you remember to ask
  • You train across modalities (run, lift, swim, mobility) and want them coordinated
  • You want a coach that knows you across years, not the next 20 minutes

What Claude is genuinely great at

Claude is one of the most useful tools ever built, and it's particularly strong on careful reasoning and nuanced conversation. For fitness, it shines at: thinking through tradeoffs (should I push through this calf tightness or back off), explaining concepts in depth (the science behind concurrent training interference), drafting thoughtful starting plans (a 4-day upper/lower split for a 35-year-old beginner with a tight schedule), and working through injury questions carefully.

If you're a self-directed athlete who values careful reasoning and just wants a sparring partner with depth, Claude is excellent.

Where it falls short: no memory of your training

Claude's Projects feature is a real step up from a stateless chat — you can keep instructions, reference documents, and conversation history within a project. What it can't hold is a structured plan: the sessions you've done, the ones coming up, your trend lines, your last conversation about how your sleep has been.

Even within a Project, every new conversation starts close to fresh from Claude's perspective; you're the one maintaining what matters. A coach holds the plan so you don't have to.

The stranger every time problem

A real coach builds a model of you over months and years. They know that your knee acts up after high-mileage weeks, that you get nervous before race blocks, that you're more honest about recovery on Sunday nights than Monday mornings. That kind of knowledge accumulates.

With Claude, even with Projects, the memory is documents you've written. It isn't a structured profile that the system can reason about. You can paste a summary, but it's a summary, not a relationship. Justus is designed to build the relationship.

Coaching is more than the plan

People think they want a plan. Mostly what they want is a coach. The plan is the artifact. The coach is the thing that changes the plan when life happens. The plan without the adaptation is just a worksheet.

Claude will draft you an excellent worksheet, with careful reasoning behind every choice. The first time you miss a Tuesday and skip a Saturday, the worksheet is wrong, and there's no one to fix it. Justus reshapes the plan when you tell it what happened — without you having to redraft anything.

Can you make Claude your coach with Projects?

Closer than you can with most general-purpose AI. A Claude Project can hold your training history, your goals, your constraints, and a custom system prompt that tells Claude how to coach you. Some people do this and get real value from it.

What still breaks: data ingestion is manual, the plan is a document you have to read and remember, there's no follow-up unless you initiate it, and Claude still re-anchors on whatever's in the project rather than building on real structured history. You become the plumbing. An app like Justus exists so you don't have to be.

When to use which, honestly

Use Claude for the careful one-off stuff: thinking through an injury question, understanding a training concept in depth, drafting a starting plan you'll manage yourself. It's a great sparring partner.

Use Justus (or any purpose-built AI coach) when you want the plan, the memory, the adaptation, and the follow-up bundled into a single product designed for the job. Different tools for different jobs.

The week-six problem in a real marathon block

You asked Claude to build a marathon plan. By week six, it does not reliably know which long run you completed, what your watch recorded, or how the missed Tuesday session changes the next three weeks. You paste the history again, and the plan is effectively rebuilt from the latest conversation.

That is the gap between a smart chatbot and a coach that holds the block. Justus keeps the race date, the plan, your completed training, and your strength work in one persistent system, then adapts the weeks ahead without asking you to become the record keeper.

Common questions about this comparison.

Can Claude actually build me a workout plan?

Yes, it can draft one. Tell Claude your goal, your schedule, and your constraints, and it'll produce a structured plan that's roughly in line with the evidence. Claude's longer context window and careful reasoning often produce a more nuanced first draft than other general-purpose models.

The catch is that the next time you open Claude, the conversation is gone unless you set up Projects. The plan only updates if you remember to re-explain everything.

Can Claude be my personal trainer?

For one-off questions, yes. For ongoing coaching, no. A trainer holds your plan, sees your data, remembers your history, and follows up. Claude does none of those by default.

Claude Projects let you keep a workspace with persistent context, which is closer than most general assistants get, but it's still a workspace — not a coach with a structured plan, wearable sync, and proactive follow-up.

Is there an AI fitness coach that's better than Claude for fitness?

Yes. Apps built specifically as AI coaches hold a persistent profile, plug into your wearable data, maintain an actual plan, and follow up over time. Justus is built that way.

Claude is a general-purpose assistant that's excellent at long-form reasoning and nuanced conversation; coaching is something it can roleplay but not actually do.

Can I make Claude remember my workouts?

Partly. Claude Projects let you store reference documents and instructions that persist across conversations within that project, which is a real step up from a stateless chat.

What it still doesn't do: hold a structured plan, sync with your watch, write to you when something needs attention, or learn from your training trajectory over years. The architecture is built for general assistance, not longitudinal coaching.

Is Justus built on Claude?

No. Justus is a multi-agent system designed specifically for fitness coaching: persistent profile, plan maintenance, multimodal training orchestration, and safety guardrails.

The underlying language models can evolve as the technology evolves — what makes Justus a coach is the architecture around the model, not which model is in the box.
