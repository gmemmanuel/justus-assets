What MyFitnessPal is actually for

MyFitnessPal is a calorie counter. That's not a slight — the food database is one of the largest in the category, the barcode scanner is fast, and the calorie math is the cleanest there is. For people whose primary goal is weight management, it's an excellent product.

The mismatch shows up when someone signs up for "fitness" and discovers that the product is really nutrition-first. The workout features are there, but they're attached, not central.

The "I've outgrown MFP" moment

A very common arc: someone joins MFP to lose 15 pounds, logs religiously for a few months, hits their goal, and then doesn't quite know what to do next. The calorie tracking has done its job. What they actually want is a coach — someone (or something) to plan what to do with the body they now have.

That's a different product. MFP isn't built to coach; Justus is.

Calories vs coaching

Calorie tracking is a measurement. It tells you what you put in and (roughly) what your body did with it. Coaching is a decision. It tells you what to do tomorrow, given everything it knows about you. One is a scale; the other is a guide.

MFP gives you the scale. Justus gives you the guide. Plenty of people benefit from both at the same time.

Can MFP also coach you?

Premium adds a library of guided workouts and lets you log exercise alongside food. It's better than nothing if you want everything in one app. But it isn't periodized, it doesn't coordinate across sports, and it doesn't adapt to your sleep or soreness or last week's actual training.

A dedicated coaching app is built differently from the ground up. The plan is the product, not a feature.

Pairing MFP and Justus, honestly

For people who care about both training and nutrition, the cleanest setup is two apps. MFP (or Cronometer, or MacroFactor) for what you eat. Justus for what you do. The overlap is minimal, which means there isn't really friction in running both.

If you want one app that does both, neither product is quite right today. The market still treats nutrition and training as separate problems.

Common questions about this comparison.

What's a good MyFitnessPal alternative?

It depends on what you've outgrown. If the part you still want is food logging, Cronometer, Lose It!, and MacroFactor are strong direct alternatives.

If what you actually want is coaching (training, not just nutrition), Justus is a better fit. MFP's core competence is calorie tracking, not workout planning.

Does MyFitnessPal include workout plans?

MyFitnessPal Premium includes a library of guided workouts and exercise logging, but it isn't a coaching app. There's no plan that adapts to your training history, no integration with your wearable beyond logging calories burned, and no follow-up.

It's a food-first product with workouts attached.

Is Justus a calorie tracker?

No. Justus is a fitness coach focused on training: planning, adaptation, multimodal orchestration, and long-term memory. Nutrition isn't part of the current research preview.

If you want both, the natural setup is a food tracker (MyFitnessPal, Cronometer, MacroFactor) for nutrition and Justus for training.

Can I use MyFitnessPal and Justus together?

Yes, and that's a sensible setup. They cover different sides of the same goal: MyFitnessPal handles intake (food, calories, macros) while Justus handles training (planning, adaptation, recovery). The overlap is minimal.

What's the difference between MyFitnessPal and a fitness coaching app?

MyFitnessPal is built around food logging — the calorie database is the product. A coaching app is built around training plans that adapt to you over time.

They live on different sides of the diet-vs-training divide. Most people who want both end up using two apps.
