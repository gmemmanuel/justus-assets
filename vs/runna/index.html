Who each one is actually for.

Runna and Justus overlap on "I want a structured running plan," and diverge sharply on everything else. Here's the most honest version of who picks which.

  • You like pace-target adaptation and structured workouts

What Runna is genuinely great at

Runna is the cleanest race-focused running app available. The plans are written by accomplished coaches, the workouts push to your watch, the pace progression is sensible, and the structured-workout experience is among the best in the category. For a runner who has a marathon in October and wants to be told what to do every day until then, Runna is hard to beat.

Calling it "running-first" isn't a knock. It's the design intent, and it's executed well.

The running-first tradeoff

Most people don't only run. You might lift twice a week, swim on Sundays, do mobility on Tuesdays, or have a Saturday soccer game. Runna treats those as around the running plan, not woven into it. The product can't easily know that yesterday's heavy back squat is the reason today's tempo feels off.

Justus's plan is the union of everything you do. The long run won't land the day after a heavy lower-body session unless you want it to.

Conversational vs template-driven planning

Runna's plans are templates that adapt mostly through pace targets — you progress, the paces shift. If you miss two weeks because of work or a calf strain, the template doesn't really know what to do. You can shift things manually, but the underlying structure is fixed.

Justus reshapes the plan conversationally. Tell it you slept three hours, that your knee is sore, that you're flying to Tokyo on Wednesday, and the next two weeks rewrite. The plan bends around your life, not the other way around.

Single-sport vs multimodal periodization

Periodization — the long arc of base, build, peak, and taper — is straightforward when you only run. It gets harder when you also lift, because lifting has its own arc, and the two interfere with each other. Most apps solve this by ignoring the other arc.

Justus is built to reason across arcs. The strength plan deloads when the run plan is peaking. The mobility work moves to match. The whole calendar is one document, not three.

Should you pair them?

Probably not. Runna and Justus are both training-plan products, which means running two of them at once means two plans — and that's worse than one. If you're race-focused on running alone and Runna serves you, stay there. If you want the coordination across modalities, switch to Justus and let it handle the running too.

The pairing that does make sense: Runna for the race block, Justus for the rest of the year when you're not chasing a specific time on the road.

Common questions about this comparison.

What's a good Runna alternative?

It depends what you want. If you want another running-focused plan app, Nike Run Club, Garmin Coach, TrainingPeaks, and Final Surge are direct comparables.

If you want a coach that handles running alongside strength, swimming, and mobility in one plan, Justus is the right fit. Runna is purpose-built for runners chasing a race; Justus is purpose-built for athletes training across modalities.

Is Runna better than Justus for marathon training?

For a runner whose entire focus is the marathon and who wants a template plan written by elite coaches with pace-target adaptation, Runna is excellent.

Justus is better if your training has to fit alongside other modalities (lifting, mobility, sleep recovery, the rest of your life), and if you want a plan that adapts conversationally rather than through pace-target shifts alone.

Does Justus include running plans like Runna?

Yes. Justus can build periodized running plans for general fitness, base building, or specific race goals.

The difference is that the plan is conversational and adapts in real time, and it's coordinated with whatever else you're doing — strength, swimming, mobility — instead of treating running as the only thing on the calendar.

Can Justus train me for a specific race?

Yes. Tell Justus you have a race on a specific date and the plan re-periodizes around it. The block structure (base, build, peak, taper) is built in. You can race-focus while still keeping the rest of your training honest.

Why pick Justus over Runna?

Three reasons. First, multimodal: Justus programs strength, swimming, and mobility alongside running in a single coordinated plan, where Runna treats strength as supplementary.

Second, conversational adaptation: tell Justus you slept poorly, missed a week, or have a sore knee and the plan reshapes. Runna's adaptation is mostly pace-target shifts within a template.

Third, memory: Justus holds a profile across years; Runna is organized around the current plan.
