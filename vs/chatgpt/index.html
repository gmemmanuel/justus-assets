Compare · Justus vs ChatGPT

ChatGPT can write you a plan. It can't really coach you.

ChatGPT (and Claude, and Gemini) can absolutely draft a workout plan. People do it every day. There's a real gap, though, between drafting a plan and coaching someone through one. This page is an honest look at where general-purpose AI falls short for fitness, and what a coach actually does differently.

Quick answer

  • ChatGPT can draft a workout plan. Tell it your goal, your schedule, your constraints, and it'll write something roughly in line with the evidence. It's good at this.
  • It has no persistent memory of your training. Tomorrow it's a stranger again. You re-explain, re-paste data, and the plan resets.
  • It can't see your data. No watch sync, no plan you log against, no awareness that you did intervals on Tuesday unless you remember to mention it.
  • It can't follow up. Coaching is a conversation that continues. ChatGPT only responds when you open it.
  • Best fit for ChatGPT: one-off questions, exercise form explanations, recipe ideas, drafting a starting plan you'll manage yourself.
  • Best fit for Justus: a coach that holds the plan, sees your data, remembers you, and adapts in real time.
  • The honest take: ChatGPT is brilliant. It's just not designed to be a coach. Different job.

Who each one is actually for.

ChatGPT and a dedicated AI fitness coach aren't the same product in different packaging; they're different products entirely. Here's the most honest version of who picks which.

Pick ChatGPT if

You just want a draft

  • You're happy managing your own plan and just want a starting point
  • You have one specific question (form, exercise swap, sets/reps math)
  • You don't mind re-explaining yourself each session
  • You already use ChatGPT for everything and like having one tool
  • You don't want or need a relationship with a coach

Pick Justus if

You want an actual coach

  • You want a plan that exists between sessions, not one you redraft each time
  • You want your watch data to actually shape the plan
  • You want to be checked on, not just respond when you remember to ask
  • You train across modalities (run, lift, swim, mobility) and want them coordinated
  • You want a coach that knows you across years, not the next 20 minutes

What ChatGPT is genuinely great at

ChatGPT is one of the most useful tools ever built. For fitness, it's excellent at: explaining concepts (what's progressive overload, how do training zones work), answering one-off questions (what should I substitute for a squat if I have a knee issue), drafting starting plans (give me a 4-day upper/lower split for a 35-year-old beginner), and quick math (estimate my 1RM from this set).

If you're a self-directed athlete who likes managing your own training and just wants a smart sparring partner, ChatGPT is probably enough.

Where it falls short: no memory of your training

ChatGPT's memory feature can hold a handful of facts ("user is a 35-year-old runner, training for a half marathon, has a sore left knee"). What it can't hold is the actual plan: the sessions you've done, the ones coming up, your trend lines, your last conversation about how your sleep has been.

Every session is mostly a cold start. Even with Projects and custom instructions, you're maintaining context yourself. A coach holds the plan so you don't have to.

The stranger every time problem

A real coach builds a model of you over months and years. They know that your knee acts up after high-mileage weeks, that you get nervous before race blocks, that you're more honest about recovery on Sunday nights than Monday mornings. That kind of knowledge accumulates.

With ChatGPT, every conversation starts close to zero. You can paste a summary, but it's a summary, not a relationship. Justus is designed to build the relationship.

Coaching is more than the plan

People think they want a plan. Mostly what they want is a coach. The plan is the artifact. The coach is the thing that changes the plan when life happens. The plan without the adaptation is just a worksheet.

ChatGPT will draft you a great worksheet. The first time you miss a Tuesday and skip a Saturday, the worksheet is wrong, and there's no one to fix it. Justus reshapes the plan when you tell it what happened — without you having to redraft anything.

Can you make ChatGPT your coach with workarounds?

Sort of. You can build a Project with custom instructions, paste your data each week, ask it to track a plan in a shared document, and run a weekly check-in conversation. Some people do this and get value from it.

What breaks: the data ingestion is manual, the plan is a document you have to read and remember, there's no follow-up unless you initiate it, and the model still re-anchors on whatever's in the prompt rather than building on your real history. You become the plumbing. An app like Justus exists so you don't have to be.

When to use which, honestly

Use ChatGPT for the one-off stuff: a starting plan, an exercise you've never tried, a question about why your runs feel hard this week. It's a great sparring partner and it's not going anywhere.

Use Justus (or any purpose-built AI coach) when you want the plan, the memory, the adaptation, and the follow-up bundled into a single product designed for the job. Different tools for different jobs.

Common questions about this comparison.

Can ChatGPT actually build me a workout plan?

Yes, it can draft one. Tell ChatGPT your goal, your schedule, and your constraints, and it'll produce a structured plan that's roughly in line with the evidence.

The catch is that the next time you open ChatGPT, it doesn't remember any of it. You're starting from scratch every session, and the plan only updates if you remember to re-explain everything.

Can ChatGPT be my personal trainer?

For one-off questions, yes. For ongoing coaching, no. A trainer holds your plan, sees your data, remembers your history, and follows up. ChatGPT does none of those by default.

You can build elaborate prompts and project setups to fake it, but the system isn't designed for the job.

Is there an AI fitness coach that's better than ChatGPT for fitness?

Yes. Apps built specifically as AI coaches hold a persistent profile, plug into your wearable data, maintain an actual plan, and follow up over time. Justus is built that way.

ChatGPT is a general-purpose assistant; coaching is something it can roleplay but not actually do.

Can I make ChatGPT remember my workouts?

Partly. ChatGPT's memory feature can hold a few facts about you, and Projects let you keep a workspace with context. Neither is a substitute for a coach that holds your actual plan, sees your watch data, and writes you when something needs attention.

The architecture is built for general assistance, not longitudinal coaching.

Is Justus built on ChatGPT?

No. Justus is a multi-agent system designed specifically for fitness coaching: persistent profile, plan maintenance, multimodal training orchestration, and safety guardrails.

The underlying language models can evolve as the technology evolves — what makes Justus a coach is the architecture around the model, not which model is in the box.
